## What to Look For in a PS5 Monitor

Not every gaming monitor works well with PS5. Here are the features that actually matter:

### HDMI 2.1 – The Must-Have

PS5 outputs video through HDMI only (no DisplayPort). To get 4K at 120Hz, you need HDMI 2.1. With HDMI 2.0, you’re limited to either 4K at 60Hz or 1440p/1080p at 120Hz. Every 4K 120Hz pick in this guide has HDMI 2.1.

### VRR (Variable Refresh Rate)

PS5 supports VRR, which syncs the display’s refresh rate to the game’s frame rate. This eliminates screen tearing without the input lag of V-Sync. All monitors in this guide support VRR. If you also game on PC and want to understand the difference between adaptive sync standards, read our G-SYNC vs FreeSync comparison.

### HDR Support

Many PS5 games support HDR, but not all monitors handle it equally. OLED panels and monitors with Full Array Local Dimming (FALD) deliver the best HDR experience. Basic HDR 400 certification doesn’t provide much visible improvement. For a full breakdown of HDR tiers and what they mean in practice, see our HDR gaming monitor guide.

### 1440p Native Support

Since a system update in late 2022, PS5 supports 1440p output natively. This makes 1440p monitors a legitimate option – the console renders at 1440p instead of downscaling from 4K, resulting in a sharper image than on a 1080p display. See our resolution comparison guide for the full breakdown of what you gain and lose at each tier. Our PS5 monitor specs explainer covers the technical requirements in more detail.

### PS5 Pro Considerations

The PS5 Pro delivers more consistent 4K 120fps gameplay and supports 8K output. For PS5 Pro owners, a 4K OLED with HDMI 2.1 and VRR is the ideal pairing – you’ll see the biggest visual upgrade over the standard PS5. For more 4K options, see our best 4K gaming monitor guide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between HDMI 2.1 and HDMI 2.0 for PS5?

HDMI 2.1 supports 4K at 120Hz and has a bandwidth of 48Gbps, while HDMI 2.0 maxes out at 4K at 60Hz with 18Gbps bandwidth. For PS5, this means HDMI 2.1 unlocks the 120fps Performance Mode in supported games at full 4K resolution. With HDMI 2.0, you can still play at 4K/60fps or drop to 1440p/1080p for 120Hz. If 120fps gaming at 4K matters to you, HDMI 2.1 is essential.

### Is 4K or 1440p better for PS5?

4K gives you the sharpest image the PS5 can produce, and most games are optimized for 4K output. However, 1440p monitors are much more affordable and still look sharp at 27 inches. Since PS5 supports native 1440p output, you get a clean image without awkward downscaling. If budget is your priority, 1440p at 120Hz is a great sweet spot. If you want the best visual quality, 4K is the way to go.

### What is VRR and why does it matter for PS5?

VRR (Variable Refresh Rate) syncs your monitor’s refresh rate to the PS5’s frame output in real time. When a game runs at 90fps, the display refreshes at 90Hz. When it dips to 70fps, the display adjusts to 70Hz. This eliminates screen tearing and makes frame rate fluctuations much less noticeable. PS5 supports VRR on HDMI 2.1 displays, and most games have been patched to support it. For more on how different refresh rates affect smoothness, see our refresh rate comparison.

### Is a monitor or TV better for PS5?

It depends on how you play. Monitors offer lower input lag (faster response to your controller inputs), which is better for competitive games. They also sit at desk distance, giving you a sharp, detailed image. TVs are better for couch gaming, local co-op, and living room setups – and modern OLED TVs have caught up noticeably on input lag. If you game at a desk, a monitor is the better choice. For couch gaming, a TV works better. Our TV as gaming monitor guide covers the tradeoffs in detail.

### What does PS5 Pro add for monitor users?

PS5 Pro offers more GPU power, meaning more games can hit stable 4K at 120fps (compared to the standard PS5 which often drops resolution to maintain frame rates). It also supports PSSR (PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution) for better upscaling, and 8K output for future displays. For monitor users, the biggest benefit is more consistent 4K 120Hz performance – a 4K OLED monitor with HDMI 2.1 is the ideal pairing for PS5 Pro.

### Does HDR make a difference on PS5?

Yes, but only on monitors that can actually display it well. Basic HDR 400 certification adds minimal visible improvement. For HDR to look meaningfully better, you need either an OLED panel (infinite contrast, per-pixel dimming) or a monitor with Full Array Local Dimming and higher peak brightness (like the Sony InZone M9’s HDR 600 with FALD). Many PS5 exclusives have excellent HDR implementation, so a good HDR display is worth it.

### What size monitor is best for PS5 gaming?

For desk gaming, 27 to 32 inches is the sweet spot. At 27 inches, 4K delivers extremely sharp pixel density (163 PPI) and the screen is easy to scan at arm’s length. At 32 inches, 4K still looks sharp (about 138 PPI) and gives you a more immersive, TV-like experience at a desk. If you sit further back (3-4 feet), 32 inches is preferable. If you sit at a normal desk distance (2-3 feet), 27 inches is ideal for most gamers.
