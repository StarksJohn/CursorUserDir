Using a keyboard and mouse on PS5 is possible — but it comes with conditions worth knowing before you buy anything. PS5 supports both input types natively, though most games aren't designed for them, and some require a separate adapter to work at all.

This guide covers which games support keyboard and mouse directly, how to connect your devices, how adapters fit into the picture, and which hardware combinations are worth the money across different budgets.

## Can You Use a Keyboard and Mouse on PS5?

Yes. PS5 supports both USB and Bluetooth keyboards and mice out of the box, with no additional software required. However, what they can do depends entirely on the context you're using them in.

For general console tasks — navigating menus, typing passwords, using the browser — any standard keyboard and mouse compatible with PS5 will work immediately after plugging in. The console detects most devices automatically.

For actual gameplay, the situation is different. A game must explicitly support keyboard and mouse input for those devices to function in-game. Most PS5 titles are built around controller input, and if a game doesn't support KB+M, the devices will be recognized by the console but ignored during play.

If a game doesn't have native support, you have two options:

- Use a converter or adapter — a device that translates keyboard and mouse signals into controller input, making the PS5 treat your peripherals as a DualSense. This opens up more games but comes with trade-offs covered in the converter section below.
- Stick with the controller — the practical default for any title not on the native support list.

One distinction worth making early: connecting a keyboard for typing and using a keyboard to play are not the same thing. The first works broadly. The second depends entirely on the game.

- Which Genres Actually Benefit from KB+M on PS5?

Not every game on the native support list will feel meaningfully different with a keyboard and mouse — it depends largely on genre.

FPS and tactical shooters are where mouse input has the clearest impact. Mouse aiming is faster and more precise than thumbstick aiming in most scenarios, particularly at longer ranges. If you've played shooters on PC before, the transition back to a mouse on PS5 will feel immediately familiar.

MMOs and strategy games are a different case — it's less about speed and more about usability. Titles like Final Fantasy XIV or Company of Heroes 3 have dense interfaces built around pointing, clicking, and hotkeys. Playing them with a controller is functional but noticeably more awkward. A good gaming keyboard for PS5 makes navigation in these games substantially more comfortable.

Simulation and sandbox games like The Sims 4, Minecraft, and Planet Coaster fall into a similar category. The mouse handles spatial selection and object placement in ways a thumbstick simply wasn't designed for.

Story-driven games, racing titles, and fighting games are the exceptions. These genres are built around controller input, and KB+M rarely improves the experience — in some cases it makes it worse. For those, the DualSense remains the right tool.

## How to Connect a Keyboard and Mouse to Your PS5

Most standard USB keyboards and mice work on PS5 immediately after plugging in — no drivers, no software, no pairing codes. The console handles detection automatically and confirms it with a brief on-screen notification.

There are two connection methods: wired and wireless. Which one makes more sense depends on how and where you're gaming.

### 1. Wired Connection (USB)

Plug your keyboard and mouse into any available USB port on the PS5 — front or back. The console recognizes them within a few seconds. No further steps are needed for basic use.

Once connected, go to Settings > Accessories > Other Accessories to adjust:

- Pointer speed — how fast the mouse cursor moves
- Mouse handedness — left or right-handed button configuration
- Key repeat delay — how long before a held key starts repeating
- Key repeat rate — how fast it repeats once it starts
- Keyboard language — input language and method

These settings apply to menu navigation and browser use. In-game sensitivity is controlled separately within each game's own settings menu.

### 2. Wireless Connection (Bluetooth or 2.4GHz Dongle)

For Bluetooth:

1. Put your keyboard or mouse into Bluetooth pairing mode. The method varies by device — check the manual if you're not sure.
2. On the PS5, go to Settings > Accessories > General > Bluetooth Accessories.
3. The console scans for nearby devices. Select yours from the list when it appears.
4. Some keyboards will trigger a passkey prompt — the PS5 displays a code on screen, and you type it on the keyboard and press Enter to confirm. Not all devices require this step, but it's normal if it appears.
5. Once paired, the device reconnects automatically the next time both the console and peripheral are powered on.

For 2.4GHz dongles:

Plug the dongle into a USB port. Most 2.4GHz keyboards and mice are pre-paired to their dongle from the factory and connect without any additional steps. Some devices require a pairing button press — check your device's manual if it doesn't connect immediately.

### 3. What If Your Device Isn't Detected?

A few things to check before assuming a hardware fault:

- Some devices require proprietary software to function. If a keyboard needs a driver to work on PC, it likely won't work on PS5 either.
- Bluetooth devices that don't appear during scanning may not be compatible with PS5. Try a different device before concluding there's a console issue.
- If a USB device isn't detected, try a different port.
- If compatibility is unclear before purchasing, check online listings or product reviews specifically mentioning PS5 support — some peripherals work wired but not wirelessly, so it's worth testing both options.

## Does Using a Keyboard and Mouse on PS5 Affect Aim Assist?

Yes — and it's one of the more important things to understand before committing to a PS5 keyboard and mouse setup for competitive games.

Aim assist is disabled for keyboard and mouse users by default. It's a standard implementation across most shooters, where controller users receive a slider or toggle to adjust assistance level while KB+M users receive none. The reasoning is simple: a mouse is already more precise than a thumbstick, so adding aim assist on top of that would make the gap between input types even wider.

## Using a PS5 Keyboard and Mouse in Games That Don't Have Native Support

A converter — also called an adapter or emulator — is a small hardware device that sits between your keyboard and mouse and the PS5, translating those inputs into signals the console reads as DualSense controller input.

This matters because it bypasses the game-level restriction. A title that ignores native KB+M input has no way of blocking converter use at the hardware level — the game simply sees controller input arriving as usual.

### 1. How Converters Actually Work

Most converters connect via USB and require a licensed controller to also be plugged in — this is what authenticates the connection with the PS5. The keyboard and mouse plug into the converter itself. Setup time varies by device, but most modern converters include game profiles that pre-configure sensitivity curves and key mappings for popular titles, so you're not starting from scratch.

The experience isn't identical to native KB+M. There's a translation layer between your mouse movement and what appears on screen, which is why sensitivity calibration matters more with converters than with native input.

### 3. Ban Risk by Game — What's Actually Known

The risk of being banned for converter use depends on the game, not the PS5 system itself. Sony does not block converters at the console level. Enforcement is handled individually by game developers and their anti-cheat systems.

Here's the current picture across the most relevant titles:

- Call of Duty (Warzone / MW3):

Ricochet Anti-Cheat has actively detected and banned XIM and Cronus Zen users, particularly in competitive and ranked playlists. High-profile streamers have faced bans, and PS5 firmware updates have reduced converter functionality in some CoD titles.

- Fortnite:

Detection measures exist but enforcement has been inconsistent. The risk is present, especially in competitive modes, but bans are less frequently reported than in CoD.

- Valorant:

Vanguard anti-cheat is active on PS5. Converter use in Valorant carries the highest ban risk of any title on this list. Using an adapter in ranked play is not advisable.

- Apex Legends:

Has native KB+M support, so a converter is unnecessary. Using one anyway adds aim assist on top of mouse input — which is both a competitive integrity issue and a potential flag for anti-cheat systems.

- Destiny 2, THE FINALS, Marvel Rivals:

Native support is absent or limited, making converters the only KB+M path. Ban risk exists but is less documented than CoD or Valorant. Check each game's Terms of Service before using one in online multiplayer.

## Setting Up Your Space for PS5 Keyboard and Mouse Gaming

Using a keyboard and mouse on PS5 introduces physical setup considerations that controller gaming doesn't. A mouse needs a flat, stable surface at the right height — and that changes how your space needs to be arranged before you start playing.

### 1. Desk Gaming: The Two Variables That Actually Matter

For a desk-based PS5 gaming setup, monitor distance and elbow height are the two factors that affect comfort most directly.

Monitor distance: A mouse requires more deliberate, larger arm movements than a controller thumbstick. If your monitor is further away than a typical PC setup — which is common when a PS5 is connected to a larger display — you'll need to compensate with higher mouse sensitivity or move the monitor closer. Most PS5 players gaming with KB+M at a desk find a viewing distance of 60–90cm more comfortable than the 1–2 meter distances typical for TV-connected console setups. If you're also building out a full display arrangement, a PS5 dual-monitor setup changes the space and distance calculations further.

Elbow height: Your elbows should rest at roughly 90 degrees when your hands are on the keyboard. If your desk surface is too high or too low relative to your chair, wrist strain builds up over longer sessions. A height-adjustable gaming desk removes that variable entirely, and pairing it with an appropriately sized gaming chair keeps the 90-degree elbow angle consistent across sitting positions.

### 2. Couch Gaming: Solving the Surface Problem

Couch gaming with a keyboard and mouse on PS5 has one practical obstacle: there's no stable flat surface at the right height by default.

The most functional solutions, in order of cost and stability:

- Lap desk or lap board — a flat, rigid surface with a cushioned underside that sits across your thighs. Provides a stable platform for both keyboard and mouse. Look for one with at least 60cm of usable surface width to give the mouse room to move. This is the most common solution and works well for casual to mid-level play.
- Side table or TV tray — a small table positioned next to the couch at seat height. More stable than a lap desk for the mouse, but requires the keyboard to be in your lap or on a separate surface, which splits your setup.
- Coffee table — works if you sit on the floor or use a low couch. Not ideal for extended sessions due to forward posture.

The Razer Turret — a keyboard with a built-in retractable mouse mat — was designed specifically for this use case and is compatible with PS5. It's a niche product, but worth knowing exists if couch gaming is your primary setup.

One Note on Cable Management

Wired keyboards and mice introduce a cable run from your input devices to the PS5 that doesn't exist with a controller. For gaming desk setups this is typically straightforward — cable clips or a basic cable channel keep everything tidy. For couch setups, a wired combo creates a real cable management challenge and is one of the clearest practical arguments for choosing a 2.4GHz wireless combo.

If you're using a converter, it adds one more USB device to manage — most converters sit between the PS5 and your peripherals, so the cable management run terminates at the converter rather than at the console directly.

## FAQs

Is PS5 compatible with a keyboard and mouse?

Yes — PS5 is compatible with most standard keyboards and mice at the system level, with no drivers or additional software required. For basic use — menus, typing, browser navigation — any compatible keyboard and mouse works immediately after connecting. For in-game use, the game itself must explicitly support keyboard and mouse input, and most PS5 titles do not.

Can I use any keyboard and mouse on PS5?

Most can, but not all. Standard USB keyboards and mice work out of the box. Devices that require proprietary drivers or companion software may not function fully. Bluetooth compatibility also varies — if a device isn’t detected during pairing, it may not be supported by PS5.

What type of keyboard is compatible with PS5?

Any standard USB or 2.4GHz wireless keyboard works. This includes mechanical, membrane, and compact layouts (60%, TKL, full-size). Keyboards that rely on brand-specific software for advanced features may have limited functionality on PS5.

Is it worth getting a keyboard and mouse for PS5?

Yes — if you play supported games. In FPS titles like Fortnite or Call of Duty, a mouse offers more precise aim than a controller. For MMOs and strategy games, a keyboard improves control and navigation. For racing, fighting, or story-driven games, a controller remains the better choice.

Do wireless and Bluetooth keyboards work on PS5?

Yes, but 2.4GHz wireless works best. USB dongle devices connect instantly and offer stable performance. Bluetooth devices can be paired in settings, but compatibility varies and input lag can be slightly higher during gameplay.

Which PS5 games support keyboard and mouse?

Only a limited number. As of 2026, fewer than 30 PS5 games support keyboard and mouse natively. Popular titles include Fortnite, Call of Duty, Apex Legends, Final Fantasy XIV, Minecraft, Diablo IV, and The Sims 4. Always check in-game settings or patch notes, as support is developer-controlled.

Does using a keyboard and mouse on PS5 affect aim assist?

Yes. Native keyboard and mouse input disables aim assist. When using a converter, the PS5 still reads input as a controller — meaning aim assist remains active, which is why converters are often considered a competitive advantage.

What’s the difference between native support and using a converter?

- Native support: Direct input, no extra hardware, no aim assist
- Converter: Translates input into controller signals, expands compatibility, keeps aim assist active

Converters add flexibility but also introduce latency, complexity, and potential ban risk in competitive games.

Are keyboard and mouse converters allowed on PS5?

Technically yes, but game-dependent. Sony does not block converters at the system level. However, many competitive games classify them as unfair and may ban accounts in ranked modes. Casual and single-player use is generally safe.

Is it legal to use a converter on PS5?

Converters are not illegal, and Sony does not prohibit them at the hardware level. Whether using one violates the rules depends on each game's Terms of Service. Most competitive multiplayer games — particularly those with active anti-cheat — classify converter use as a breach of fair play rules in ranked modes, which can result in account action. Using converters in casual or single-player modes is generally not an issue.

What’s the best converter for PS5 keyboard and mouse?

It depends on your budget and use case:

- LeadJoy VX2 AimBox: budget, plug-and-play
- Beloader Pro: lower latency, better for FPS
- XIM Matrix: advanced control, highest risk in competitive games

Choose based on performance needs and tolerance for detection risk.

What settings should I use for keyboard and mouse on PS5?

Start with:

- Increase pointer speed in system settings
- Adjust sensitivity in each game (not system-wide like PC)
- Disable unnecessary mouse acceleration (in supported titles)

Fine-tuning depends on the game — FPS titles benefit most from in-game sensitivity adjustments.

Is keyboard and mouse better than controller on PS5?

For some games, yes. In FPS and strategy titles, keyboard and mouse offer better precision and control. For racing, sports, and fighting games, controllers are still the better option due to game design.

A PS5 keyboard and mouse setup works well when matched to the right games and the right expectations. Native support covers the titles where it matters most — FPS, MMO, and strategy games. Converters fill the gap for everything else, with trade-offs worth understanding before buying. For most players, one of the budget wireless combos is enough to start. For competitive use, the product and converter sections above give you everything needed to make an informed decision. Check your game's current settings before purchasing — support status changes with patches.
