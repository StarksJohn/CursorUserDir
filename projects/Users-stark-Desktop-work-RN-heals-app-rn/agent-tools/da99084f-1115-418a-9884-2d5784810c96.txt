Sometimes, an app update can cause more problems than it solves. Maybe it introduces a new bug, breaks a feature, or annoys your users. In such cases, you might want to roll back to a previous version of your app until you fix the issue. But how do you do that without losing data, users, or ratings? In this article, we'll explore some of the best strategies for rolling back an app update for both iOS and Android platforms.

## 1 Use version control

One of the most important tools for any app developer is version control. Version control allows you to track, manage, and revert changes to your code. It also helps you collaborate with other developers and avoid conflicts. You should always use version control to store your app's source code and assets, and commit changes regularly. This way, you can easily go back to a working version of your app if something goes wrong with an update. Some of the most popular version control systems are Git, Subversion, and Mercurial.

## 2 Test before releasing

Another essential strategy for avoiding the need to roll back an app update is to test it thoroughly before releasing it to the public. Testing can help you identify and fix bugs, performance issues, compatibility problems, and user feedback. You should test your app on different devices, operating systems, screen sizes, and network conditions. You should also use automated testing tools, such as unit tests, integration tests, and UI tests, to ensure your app's functionality and quality. Additionally, you should use beta testing platforms, such as TestFlight for iOS and Firebase App Distribution for Android, to distribute your app to a limited group of users and collect feedback.

## 3 Use staged rollouts

Even if you test your app extensively, you might still encounter unexpected issues when you release it to a large and diverse audience. That's why you should use staged rollouts, which allow you to gradually release your app update to a percentage of your users and monitor its performance and impact. Staged rollouts can help you reduce the risk of introducing a major bug, measure user satisfaction, and collect data for analysis. You can use staged rollouts for both iOS and Android apps, using the App Store Connect and Google Play Console respectively.

## 4 Implement remote config

Another useful strategy for rolling back an app update is to implement remote config, which allows you to change the behavior and appearance of your app without releasing a new version. Remote config enables you to modify certain parameters or features of your app from a server, and apply them to different segments of your users. For example, you can use remote config to enable or disable a feature, change the layout or color scheme, or adjust the content or language of your app. This way, you can quickly revert any changes that cause problems or dissatisfaction among your users.

## 5 Follow best practices

Finally, you should follow some best practices for rolling back an app update, depending on your platform and situation. For iOS apps, you should always keep a backup of your app's binary and metadata, and use the App Store Connect API to restore them if needed. You should also inform your users about the reason and duration of the rollback, and avoid changing the app's bundle ID or version number. For Android apps, you should use the Google Play Console to halt, resume, or revert a staged rollout, and use the versionCode and versionName attributes to manage your app's versions. You should also communicate with your users through in-app messages, notifications, or emails, and request them to update their app when you fix the issue.
